Formula

The page orders the published cam card whose RPM range covers this cubic inch, head port, power adder, and use. Street takes the shortest card that still covers the RPM. Drag takes the longest card aimed at that RPM. Overlap at 0.050 = (intake duration + exhaust duration) / 2 - (2 x lobe separation). Nitrous, turbo, and supercharger cards are separate lists.

Trackside notes

Degree it, check piston-to-valve, and match the spring and the converter to the card before you order.

  • Degree the cam and check piston-to-valve with clay. On a nitrous grind the longer exhaust lobe is the one that hits.
  • The spring has to match the lift and the RPM on the card. A stock truck spring will not hold a .600 lobe at 7,000.
  • The stall has to sit where the cam makes power. A stock converter and a cam that comes in around 2,600 leaves flat.

Will the converter pull it?

Street stays on the shorter cam so a tighter converter can leave. Drag, with the stall already in the power, can run the longer card. A 364 with a cathedral head at 7,000 RPM is Comp 54-459-11 on the street, 231/239, and 54-462-11 at the track, 243/251. Summit Stage 1, 222/233, is the largest of their stages that still works with a stock converter.

Why does the turbo pick the cam?

A small single turbo usually has more pressure in the exhaust than in the intake. Close the exhaust valve early and keep overlap near zero. Those are the Summit turbo cards. A large turbo or a pair with low drive pressure can use more overlap. Brian Tooley Stage 4 is the card for a stock-stroke engine over 8,000 RPM, and it needs the valvetrain sold with it. A factory 204-degree LS3 or LS6 cam is the wrong lobe for either one.

What does a nitrous cam change?

The exhaust lobe is longer than the intake so the shot has a way out. The BTR 5.3 card is 233/250 at 0.050, with a rough idle and a high stall. The 400-cube card is 247 on the intake and prints the exhaust as 27X. BTR calls 7,500 RPM with SP010 springs and valves under 105 grams, and 8,000 with SK703 springs and light valves. A 1-bolt engine needs the 3-bolt conversion.

Where does it stop making RPM?

Comp's hydraulic LSR cams are published to about 7,200 RPM. The 4.8 and 5.3 turbo cams, 54-330-11 and 54-332-11, go to about 7,400, and they are the wrong cam in a 6.0. Past the number on the card the lifter or the spring is done. A 408 uses the 400-cube cards.

Formula

K = Brake stall RPM / sqrt(torque at that RPM). Brake stall is set under the torque peak so the engine can accelerate through it. Stall torque ratio is as high as the tire can take. Cruise RPM = (MPH x top gear x rear gear x 336) / tire diameter, and an unlocked converter has to stall under that.

What is K-factor?

K-factor is brake stall RPM divided by the square root of engine torque at that RPM. The same converter stalls higher behind an engine with more torque. Give the shop the K, the stall, and the torque, not a stall number with no engine attached.

Where should the stall sit?

Brake stall wants to land about 500 to 700 RPM under the torque peak so the engine accelerates through the converter instead of starting above it. A no-prep tire wants it lower. An unlocked street converter also has to stall under cruise RPM.

Why is the car weight not in the stall?

Brake stall is engine torque against the converter with the turbine held. Weight, the tire, and the gear decide whether that stall hooks. They do not change the RPM.

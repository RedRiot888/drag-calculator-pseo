Formula

Air lb/min = (Flywheel HP x BSFC x Power AFR) / 60. Required boost comes from that airflow divided by the engine's naturally aspirated flow at your RPM and volumetric efficiency.

Trackside notes

Match the compressor to the pounds per minute you will run, then see if the intercooler can pull the outlet temperature down.

  • Read the compressor map at the boost you race, not at the choke line.
  • A boost number that is short of the horsepower target means the engine runs out of air first.
  • Outlet temperature is what the intercooler has to pull out. Pressure ratio and efficiency set it.

How do you match a turbo?

Match airflow in pounds per minute and the pressure ratio to a compressor map, then check the island at the boost you will actually run.

Why can the typed boost miss the power number?

Naturally aspirated flow at that RPM and volumetric efficiency sets how much boost the horsepower target needs. A short boost number runs out of air first.

What does compressor outlet temperature tell you?

Outlet temperature rises with pressure ratio and falls as compressor efficiency improves. The intercooler has to pull that heat out before the intake valve.

Formula

Pass heat = HP x BSFC x fuel BTU/lb x 0.06 x seconds / 3600. That 6% is the share that stays in the metal and the water on a short pass. Fan CFM = BTU/hr / (1.08 x air rise). Pump GPM = BTU/min / (coolant rise x lb/gal x specific heat).

Trackside notes

  • Density altitude: about 3% naturally aspirated power per 1,000 feet.
  • Converter slip at the finish is often kept around 5% to 8%.
  • On boost, pull roughly 1 to 2 degrees of timing for every 3 to 4 PSI.

Why is a drag radiator not sized like a dyno?

A dyno pull holds the power. A pass is about 10 seconds, and most of the fuel energy leaves in the exhaust before it ever reaches the water. The temperature climb on one hit is the check.

When does the fan actually work?

Ram air does the return road. The fan works in the lanes, and only if a shroud makes it pull through the core. Leaving the engine running is a bigger load than the pass itself.

Why an electric water pump if the car already has one?

A mechanical pump moves the most water at RPM, which is the hit. It moves little at idle. The electric pump is there so the fans still have flow while the car sits.

Formula

CID = Bore x Bore x Stroke x 0.7854 x 8. SCR = (Swept volume + clearance volume) / clearance volume. Clearance volume is the chamber, the gasket, the deck, and the piston dish. DCR uses the piston position at intake-valve closing instead of the full stroke.

Trackside notes

The bore, the stroke, and the piston height have to clear. The cam is a separate card.

  • A 4.000 inch crank in an iron 5.3 or 6.0 is the budget stroker, if the compression height still clears the rings.
  • Dynamic compression uses the piston position when the intake valve closes. Blank stays at 40 degrees so the ratio has a number.
  • An LS7 crank shares the mains, and an LS3 head bolts to a 6.0. The chamber versus the bore still has to match.

How does a stroker change the inches?

Displacement is bore times bore times stroke times 0.7854 times 8. A 4.000 inch crank in an iron 5.3 or 6.0 adds cubes without changing the bore, as long as the piston compression height still clears the rings.

What is the difference between static and dynamic compression?

Static compression uses the full stroke. Dynamic compression uses the piston position when the intake valve closes, so a later-closing cam bleeds some of that compression off.

Why can a part fit the block and still be the wrong part?

An LS7 crank shares the main journal, and an LS3 head bolts to a 6.0, but the piston height, the chamber versus the bore, and the power limit still have to match the combination.

Where is the cam?

This build does not pick a lobe. Dynamic compression uses 40 degrees after bottom dead center until you type the intake close from the cam card. The cam calculator orders the card for the head, the cubic inches, and the power adder.

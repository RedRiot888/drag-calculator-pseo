Formula

Critical RPM = 4,770,000 x material x square root(OD^2 + ID^2) / length^2, for a steel tube. Aluminum uses 0.981 of that, from the modulus and the density. The planning limit is 75% of critical. Shaft RPM = engine RPM / top gear. Working angle is the difference between the tube and the transmission, and between the tube and the pinion.

  • Density altitude: about 3% naturally aspirated power per 1,000 feet.
  • Converter slip at the finish is often kept around 5% to 8%.
  • On boost, pull roughly 1 to 2 degrees of timing for every 3 to 4 PSI.

Where is the driveshaft measured?

At ride height, center of the front U-joint to center of the rear U-joint. The slip yoke wants about 3/4 in before it bottoms at ride, and about 1 in still engaged at full droop.

What is critical speed?

It is the RPM where the tube's first bending mode lines up with the shaft speed. The constant 4,770,000 is the steel modulus and density in that formula. Stay under 75% of it. A bigger diameter raises it. A longer tube lowers it by the length squared.

Why can the pinion angle look wrong at rest?

A 4-link barely wraps, so the pinion should already point along the tube. A leaf spring wraps on the launch, so it wants about 2 degrees nose down at rest and straight once the axle winds up.

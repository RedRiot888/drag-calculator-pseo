What does the front extension knob do?

On the launch the front shock gets longer. That circuit is extension. More extension slows the rise and softens the hit. Less extension lets the nose come up faster and plants the tire harder.

Why is rear compression the hit knob?

The rear shock gets shorter as the car squats. More compression keeps the tire from getting shocked. Rear extension is the recovery and the gear change, not the first hit. A single adjustable cannot move compression.

How do prepped, radial prep, and no prep differ?

A prepped slick can take a faster hit, so front extension and rear compression stay softer. Radial prep is the middle. No prep breaks loose if the hit is sharp, so those two knobs go firmer. The shock cannot invent grip the track does not have.

Which shock do I order?

Measure center to center at ride, at full bump, and at full droop. Order a collapsed length at least 1/2 in shorter than the bump measurement and an extended length at least 1/2 in longer than droop. The front wants more of that stroke in extension. The rear wants more of it in compression.
